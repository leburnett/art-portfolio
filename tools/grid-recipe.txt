GRID RECIPE — how the eight landing-page tile crops were made
=============================================================

Sources live outside this repo, in:

    ~/Documents/ART_PORTFOLIO/grid_images/

Each command writes to a scratch folder; copy the result over the tile file it
replaces. Run them from the repo root. SRC and OUT are set once:

    SRC=~/Documents/ART_PORTFOLIO/grid_images
    OUT=/tmp/gridbuild && mkdir -p $OUT


OIL PASTELS  (left tile)
------------------------
All four are plain centred crops — the work fills the frame, so there is
nothing to balance.

    python3 tools/prepare_images.py --grid --no-watermark -o $OUT \
        $SRC/oil1.jpg $SRC/oil2.jpg $SRC/oil3.jpg $SRC/oil4.jpg

    cp $OUT/oil1.jpg assets/images/tile-pastel-1.jpg
    cp $OUT/oil2.jpg assets/images/tile-pastel-2.jpg
    cp $OUT/oil3.jpg assets/images/tile-pastel-3.jpg
    cp $OUT/oil4.jpg assets/images/tile-pastel-4.jpg


RELIEF PRINTING  (right tile)
-----------------------------
Each one carries its own shift. See WHY below.

    python3 tools/prepare_images.py --grid --no-watermark --hshift  0.10  -o $OUT $SRC/lino1.HEIC
    python3 tools/prepare_images.py --grid --no-watermark --vshift -0.068 -o $OUT $SRC/lino2.HEIC
    python3 tools/prepare_images.py --grid --no-watermark --vshift  0.045 -o $OUT $SRC/lino3.HEIC
    python3 tools/prepare_images.py --grid --no-watermark                 -o $OUT $SRC/lino4.HEIC

    cp $OUT/lino1.jpg assets/images/tile-relief-1.jpg
    cp $OUT/lino2.jpg assets/images/tile-relief-2.jpg
    cp $OUT/lino3.jpg assets/images/tile-relief-3.jpg
    cp $OUT/lino4.jpg assets/images/tile-relief-4.jpg


WHY THE SHIFTS EXIST
--------------------
The white title band lies across the middle of each tile and covers about 11%
of every cell: the BOTTOM of the two top cells, the TOP of the two bottom ones.
So a perfectly centred crop looks off-centre once it is on the page — top cells
appear to sit low, bottom cells appear to sit high.

    lino1  --hshift 0.10    owl, top-left. Nothing to do with the band: the
                            crop window moves left so more of the owl and less
                            of the tree strip is in frame.

    lino2  --vshift -0.068  bird, top-right. The band hides its bottom, so the
                            subject is lifted. Measured result: 31px of paper
                            above, 33px below.

    lino3  --vshift  0.045  blue tit, bottom-left. The band hides its top, so
                            the subject is dropped. Measured: 21px above,
                            21px below.

    lino4  none             inktober sheet, bottom-right. Fills its cell edge
                            to edge, so there is no surrounding paper to
                            balance.


IF YOU REPLACE A PHOTO, RE-MEASURE
----------------------------------
These numbers belong to the PHOTOGRAPH, not to the position in the grid. When
lino2 was reshot, the same slot went from needing -0.038 to needing -0.068,
because the new frame was a different shape (3514x3529 against 2886x3070).

The way to find a new value: process it plain first, look at the page, and
nudge. Positive vshift puts the subject lower, negative higher. Positive
hshift keeps more of the left of the original. Roughly 0.045 moves a subject
about 27px on screen at a 1512px-wide window.

--cell top / --cell bottom applies the band correction automatically using the
measured 11%. It is the right starting point; --vshift is for when you want it
exact.


TWO THINGS THAT CAN DRIFT
-------------------------
1. BAND_HIDE = 0.111 in prepare_images.py is measured from a rendered 296px
   cell with 33px covered. Change the band's padding or font size and that
   number is wrong, and --cell will over- or under-correct.

2. A shift costs a little zoom. When the crop already fills one axis of the
   original there is no slack to move into, so the box shrinks to make room.
   On a photo with generous paper margins that is an improvement; on one
   already cropped tight it starts eating into the print.
